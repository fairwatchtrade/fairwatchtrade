# Authenticated Functional Copy Law

Status: GOVERNING
Applies to: every authenticated functional surface — Account workspaces,
collector workspaces, settings, operational rooms, and any transactional or
functional UI copy addressed to the signed-in person.

## Core Law

> **In authenticated functional copy, never describe the reader in third
> person. Address the reader directly; name the other party only when their
> role is necessary. Editorial, marketing, manifesto, and public listing copy
> are outside this rule.**

## Why this is a law and not a preference

A signed-in person reading their own workspace is present. Copy that says
"requests from collectors you may answer" or "the collector's exact budget"
is written *about* somebody to a third party — and the person it is about is
the one reading it. It makes a private room sound like a description of the
room, and it makes the product sound like it is discussing its users rather
than serving the one in front of it.

It is also easy to violate accidentally: the role noun arrives naturally
while writing, reads as precise, and survives review because nothing about
it is factually wrong. Only the footing is wrong.

## Required Behavior

- Address the reader as **you**.
- Name the other side (buyer, seller, the person who asked) **only** where
  their role carries meaning the sentence needs — privacy, authority, whose
  action is whose.
- Prefer the plain noun for what is being discussed over the role noun for
  the person discussing it: *watches people are looking for*, not
  *collector demand*.

## Forbidden Behavior

- Narrating the signed-in reader as a role: "sellers can…", "the collector
  sees…", where the reader IS that seller or collector.
- Repeating a role noun that the room's own context already supplies.
- Using internal system vocabulary in a sentence addressed to a person
  (*governed inventory*, *projection*, *entitlement*) when a plain phrase
  states the same truth.

## Out of Scope

Editorial prose, marketing, manifestos, public listing copy, and public brand
voice are outside this rule, and do not become subject to it merely because
they contain pronouns. This law does not ban third-person language about
someone else when that person's role is necessary to explain privacy,
authority, or an action.

## Worked examples from the surfaces that prompted it

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Open requests from collectors you may be able to answer… | Watches people are looking for that you may be able to answer… |
| The collector's exact budget and identity are never shown here. You are told only… | You're never shown their exact budget or who they are — only whether… |
| Tell FairWatchTrade what you are actively trying to buy. Eligible sellers can answer with governed inventory… | Tell us what you're looking to buy. Sellers can see if they already have a match and answer with that listing… |

In the third example the other party is still named, because "sellers" is
what the sentence is about: it explains who answers. That is the rule working,
not an exception to it.
